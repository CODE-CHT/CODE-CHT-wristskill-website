How much protein is in that?

By WristSkill Labs, LLC · September 4, 2026 · Values from USDA FoodData Central, rounded to the nearest gram.

Logging protein is only as accurate as the numbers you punch in. These are the foods people ask about most, with protein per common serving from the USDA's FoodData Central — the federal food-composition database that nutrition labels and tracking apps ultimately trace back to. Values are rounded; exact figures vary slightly by cut, brand, and preparation, so treat the label in your hand as the tiebreaker.

The three details that change your numbers

Cooked vs. raw weight. Cooking drives off water, so protein concentrates: about 4 oz of raw chicken becomes about 3 oz cooked with the same protein inside. Neither convention is wrong — just pick one and log it consistently.

Eggs don't care how you cook them. USDA lists a large egg at ~6.3 g raw, ~6.3 g fried, and ~6.2 g poached — functionally identical. Cook them however you like them.

Scoops aren't standardized. A "scoop" of protein powder ranges from the low 20s to 30+ grams of protein depending on the product and scoop size. For powders and shakes, the label always beats a generic table — including this one.

The usual reminder: this is a table of food facts, not nutrition advice — it says what's in foods, not what or how much you should eat. Talk to a physician, dietitian, or other qualified health provider before changing your diet.
