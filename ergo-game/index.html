How it works

This is what the pilot version is being built to do.

  1. An invite in Microsoft Teams

    Your Teams admin approves the app once. After that, people join from a message in Teams, with nothing to install on their computer. Slack is planned for later.

  2. A 10-minute setup quest

    Chair, keyboard and mouse, monitor, laptop and lighting. Each mission says what to check, how to fix it with what you already have, and what to buy if that doesn't work.

  3. A check-in three days later

    "Still comfortable?" If the fix held, the mission is complete. If it didn't, the game offers a different fix.

  4. Short breaks on one shared map

    Quick movement and position-change breaks spread through the workday, each under two minutes, with an easier version and a skip that still counts. Everyone's progress moves the team across the same map. Goals are weekly, so a missed day costs nothing, and there is no individual leaderboard.

What HR can see

These are the privacy rules the pilot is being built to. Employees will be able to open the same list inside the game, so they know exactly what their employer gets.

HR sees

  • How many people were invited, signed up, and took part each week
  • Team totals, and only for groups of 5 or more people

HR doesn't see

  • Any one person's answers, setup or progress
  • Pain or symptom reports. The game is designed without health questions, and its "this hurts" button is designed to give private advice without recording anything.
  • Camera, keystroke or screen activity. None of those are part of the design.
